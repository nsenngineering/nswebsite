# Goal 6: AI Search Visibility (GEO)

## Objective

The purpose of this test was to determine whether AI-powered search and retrieval systems can discover and accurately describe **N.S. Engineering & Geotechnical Services Pvt. Ltd. (NS Engineering)** for realistic prospective-client queries.

The test focused on category-level queries rather than only branded searches because category queries provide a better indication of whether NS Engineering is discoverable by a potential client who does not already know the company.

The four primary queries were:

1. "Who does pile testing in Nepal?"
2. "What is PDA testing and who provides it in Kathmandu?"
3. "Best geotechnical engineering firm in Nepal"
4. "Who does soil investigation for hydropower projects in Nepal?"

The intended test surfaces were:

1. ChatGPT with web search enabled
2. Perplexity with web search enabled
3. Claude with web search enabled
4. Google Search / AI Overview
5. Google AI Mode

At the time of this report, results have been collected for ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, and Google AI Search/AI Overview.

---

# 1. Testing Methodology

## 1.1 Query selection

The queries were selected to represent realistic questions that potential NS Engineering clients might ask.

Three of the four queries are strongly category-oriented:

- pile testing in Nepal
- PDA testing in Kathmandu
- soil investigation for hydropower projects in Nepal

The fourth query asks for the "best" geotechnical engineering firm and therefore tests whether NS Engineering is considered among notable firms rather than simply whether its brand is retrieved.

No query explicitly included "NS Engineering", allowing the test to measure unprompted visibility.

## 1.2 Evaluation criteria

For every query/surface combination, the following were evaluated:

- Whether NS Engineering appeared at all
- Whether it appeared without being named in the query
- Its relative prominence in the answer
- Whether it appeared as an early/primary recommendation or lower in the list
- Whether the information was relevant to the question
- Whether the claims appeared consistent with NS Engineering's published information
- Which competing companies appeared when NS Engineering was absent or ranked lower
- What type of information competitors appeared to have that could explain their visibility

AI-generated claims were not automatically treated as factual. Claims were considered candidates for verification against NS Engineering's website and other authoritative sources.

## 7. Content Audit and GEO Improvement Opportunities
### 7.1 Project Content Audit

The project data was reviewed to determine whether NS Engineering's project pages contain concrete, independently verifiable information that an AI retrieval system could extract and cite.

The audit found that the website contains a substantial amount of useful project-specific information, but the level of specificity varies considerably between projects.

Several projects contain strong citable facts, including project capacity, location, drilling depth, named testing methods, and test counts. Other project records describe the investigation only in broad terms such as "geotechnical investigation" or "rock testing" without providing measurable details.

The strongest examples include:

- Nalgad Storage HPP (417 MW): drilling to 284 m, four in-situ direct shear tests, and nine plate-jacking tests.
- Upper Bheri PRoR HEP (325 MW): ERT, SRT and MASW geophysical surveys.
- Upper Arun PRoR HEP (1,063.36 MW): block shear, plate-jacking and dilatometer testing.
- Mewa Khola HEP (50 MW): laboratory testing, liquefaction analysis and bearing-capacity assessment.
- Thulo Khola HPP (21.3 MW): in-situ Lugeon testing.
- West Seti HEP (750 MW): PIT drilling, pressure-meter, plate-load and shear testing.

These examples provide stronger factual signals than generic marketing statements because they contain specific project capacities, technical methods and measurable quantities.

### 7.2 Main Content Gap

The main weakness is inconsistency.

Some project pages provide detailed technical information, while others only state that geotechnical investigation, drilling, sampling or testing was performed.

For example, statements such as:

"Supported detailed geotechnical investigation"

are less useful to an AI retrieval system than a statement identifying the actual investigation method, test type, project capacity or measurable result.

Where the information is publicly verified and approved for publication, project pages should therefore be expanded with concrete facts rather than additional generic marketing language.

### 7.3 Recommended Content Pattern

Future project pages should follow a consistent structure:

- Project name
- Project capacity
- Location
- Client
- Investigation scope
- Drilling method and depth, where verified
- Sampling method, where verified
- Laboratory tests, where publicly stated
- Rock-mechanics tests, where publicly stated
- Geophysical methods, where applicable
- Specific analysis performed
- Project outcome, where publicly documented
- Source/evidence

Information should only be added when it can be verified from project records or another authoritative source.

### 7.4 GEO Content Priority

The first pages to improve should be projects most closely related to the queries tested in this experiment, particularly hydropower geotechnical investigation.

Priority should be given to projects containing strong technical evidence such as:

1. Nalgad Storage HPP
2. Upper Arun PRoR HEP
3. Upper Bheri PRoR HEP
4. Mewa Khola HEP
5. West Seti HEP
6. Phukot Karnali PRoR HEP

These projects provide concrete evidence that can support future AI answers to questions such as:

"Who does soil investigation for hydropower projects in Nepal?"

and:

"Which companies provide geotechnical investigation for hydropower projects in Nepal?"
